# Simplified bubble-simulation workflow and interface coordinates

The original conceptual figures are generated by
`scripts/create_hybrid_workflow_figures.py`. They describe the workflow specified
in the mass-transfer manuscript, not an executed CFD calculation or a measured
dataset. The generator uses `notebooks/figure_style.py` and
`notebooks/figure_utils.py`, and reuses the tested `vof_geometry` function from
`scripts/create_interface_model_figures.py` for its first-stage icon.

## Reproduction and exports

From the repository root:

```bash
MPLCONFIGDIR=/tmp/ml-hybrid-mpl .venv-quarto/bin/python \
  scripts/create_hybrid_workflow_figures.py --output-dir notebooks/media
```

A new, initially empty directory can replace `notebooks/media` for independent
reproduction. No external datasets or additional Python packages beyond NumPy
and Matplotlib are needed. Both stems have light and dark SVG, PDF, and 300 DPI
PNG exports:

| Stem | Exact physical size |
| --- | --- |
| `bubble-hybrid-workflow` | 180 × 74 mm |
| `bubble-interface-coordinates` | 180 × 118 mm |

The shared named width is 180 mm. The four horizontal stages use 10 pt headings and 9 pt labels.
Small icon labels and supporting annotations are 10 pt. Deliberate exceptions
are the 8 pt angular/time ticks under the miniature function icons and the 9 pt
conceptual-status footer in the coordinate figure. The short labels leave the detailed explanation to the lecture text. Spatial diagrams
retain equal aspect ratio. Their coordinate lengths and vector magnitudes are
arbitrary sketch units, not quantitative predictions.

The production renderer is LaTeX (`text.usetex=True`), tested by actual
representative SVG and PNG smoke exports before producing the set. Smoke files
are written into a temporary directory. The explicit `--no-tex` option uses
MathText; there is no silent fallback. That option is tested separately and is
not used for production exports. Text becomes glyph paths in SVG; its editable
LaTeX source remains in the generator.

Light backgrounds are white, dark backgrounds are `#111111`. Geometry, label
placement, function profiles, vector components, cell fractions, dimensions,
and limits are identical across the pair. Categorical accents retain the
standard C0–C9 colors after each theme is applied. The VOF scalar icon retains
the same sequential `viridis` map and [0, 1] limits in both themes.

## Four-stage workflow

1. **Transient two-phase simulation.** The VOF icon uses actual geometric
   fractions for a conceptual ellipse on a coarse 12 × 9 fixed grid. Local PLIC
   segments reproduce those fractions. Red markers show example sample locations on the PLIC segments; they are not observations, fitted data, or a temporal
   convergence study. The full geometric construction and checks are documented
   in `figures/interface-models/README.md`.
2. **Continuous regression models.** A hand-defined rise-speed history starts
   at rest. The other two icons show angular profiles at a fixed time `t_*`,
   with ticks at 0, pi, and 2 pi. The radius is the analytic polar radius of
   the oblate ellipse: `r = [(sin(theta)/A)^2 + (cos(theta)/B)^2]^(-1/2)`.
   The signed tangential velocity is proportional to `sin(theta)`, so it is
   zero at both poles and reverses sign on the opposite meridian. All profiles
   are illustrative, not fitted or measured; angular profiles represent a
   nonzero time, while the manuscript supplies their initial-time factors.
3. **Liquid-only moving mesh.** Only the exterior of the ellipse is meshed.
   The interior matches the page background and is explicitly labeled as a
   void with the gas excluded. The arrow at the inner boundary denotes
   deformation; the downward arrow denotes `-Ub(t) ez` in the translating
   bubble frame. Radius, rise speed, and tangential liquid velocity provide
   different parts of the boundary specification. The illustrated exterior
   annular mesh is conceptual and does not represent a complete solver mesh.
4. **Transient liquid transport.** The final stage solves time-dependent liquid
   flow and dissolved-species transport. Curved blue arrows symbolize liquid
   motion; a single orange shaded region follows a surface layer into the
   downstream wake. It is a conceptual transport region, without numerical
   concentration values or quantitative boundary-layer thickness. Nested
   concentric contours are deliberately omitted.


## Coordinates and velocity decomposition

The meridional slice assumes an axisymmetric, star-shaped interface. Its polar
angle `vartheta` is measured from the positive vertical direction and increases
toward the right-hand bubble side, from 0 to pi. The displayed ellipse has
horizontal and vertical semiaxes 0.94 and 0.76 in arbitrary sketch units. Its
polar radius is evaluated analytically, rather than inferred from a drawn arrow.

The outward radial direction is `er = (sin(vartheta), cos(vartheta))` in the
displayed horizontal–vertical coordinates. The increasing-angle tangent and
outward normal are calculated from the radial function and its angle derivative:

```text
tSigma = (r_vartheta er + r e_vartheta) / sqrt(r² + r_vartheta²)
nSigma = (r er - r_vartheta e_vartheta) / sqrt(r² + r_vartheta²)
```

The normal points from gas `Omega^-(t)` into liquid `Omega^+(t)`. The local
inset uses these same orthonormal directions at `vartheta = 0.8`; a conceptual
parabolic surface segment has the corresponding tangent at the marked point.
Its curvature is not a measured quantity. The two component arrows and dotted
parallelogram add to the relative-velocity arrow. Arbitrary positive component
lengths were chosen for readability. In particular, positive outward normal
velocity can represent local expansion/deformation; it is not evidence of
net phase change or bubble-volume loss.

The displayed relative velocity subtracts **bubble translation** from the
laboratory-frame liquid velocity at the interface. It does not subtract the
full mesh velocity. In the manuscript's negligible-phase-change setting,
normal liquid velocity equals normal boundary motion:

```text
u_rel = u_lab|Sigma - Ub(t) ez = ut tSigma + un nSigma
un = (partial_t r er) dot nSigma
```

Thus the normal component comes from the evolving geometry and is not an
independently fitted velocity. The mesh and liquid need not share a tangential
velocity. This simplified normal condition is not asserted for a general
interface with appreciable phase-change mass flux.

## Source context and checks

The manuscript provides the specific hybrid workflow, governing equations,
regression ansatz, and its assumptions. General method context is provided by
[Roenby et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.160405) for geometric VOF and
[Tukovic and Jasak (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compfluid.2011.11.003) for
moving-mesh interface tracking. These figures do not reproduce published data
or assert that their illustrative workflow was executed in those papers.

Production exports were generated in an initially empty temporary directory.
The actual light/dark exports were inspected for clipping, label overlaps,
readability, arrow directions, and whether the excluded interior remains empty.
The first local-inset fill exposed an artificial closing edge; that edge was
moved outside the visible panel and the result was re-exported. The polar-angle
and time-dependent-domain notation matches the manuscript.

Analytic checks establish orthogonality of the tangent and outward normal and
the intended signs. At the illustrated point, their dot product is 0 to the
reported floating-point precision, while `er dot nSigma = 0.9785321866`.
The increasing-angle tangent has positive projection onto `e_vartheta`.
The reused VOF geometry has 26 mixed cells, maximum PLIC-fraction discrepancy
`1.89e-15`, and total ellipse-area sampling error `9.80e-8` relative. These are
illustration-consistency checks, not CFD or surrogate-model validation.

Actual Quarto-page theme visibility, loading paths, source-download consistency,
and apparent type size after page scaling are checked during manuscript
integration; the standalone generator does not perform browser checks.
